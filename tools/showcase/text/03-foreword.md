# Foreword

The season opened eleven days late. The winter gales had taken the top two
courses of stone off the landing wall, and until the wall was rebuilt there
was nowhere on the island a loaded boat could come alongside without a swell
under it. Nobody was hurt, no equipment was lost, and the wall is better now
than it has been since the trust bought the place. But eleven days at the
front of a season are not eleven days in the middle of one, and the two
weeks we lost were the two weeks the first passage birds use the island.

What that means in practice is written into every table in this review, and
it would be dishonest to leave the reader to discover it in the numbers. The
spring totals are low because we were not there to count, not because the
birds did not come.

> A gap in a record is not a small number. It is no number at all, and a
> table that does not say which of the two it is holding has stopped being a
> record and become a decoration.

The rest of the season went well. The new gauge in the sound has run without
a break since the eighth week. The bunkhouse took forty-one visitors across
nineteen separate stays, which is the most since the trust started keeping
count, and not one of them arrived without a permit, which is the first time
that has ever been true.

We put that down to the form. It used to be a sheet of paper that had to be
printed, filled in by hand, photographed on a kitchen table and sent back as
four unreadable pictures. It is now two pages that can be filled in on the
machine the visitor is already sitting at, signed there, and sent back as one
file. The change cost the trust nothing and saved the warden about a day a
month, which over a season is a fortnight of somebody's life given back.

## What comes next

The roof of the store is the next thing. It is not leaking yet. It is the
kind of roof that does not leak for years and then leaks everywhere in one
night, and the trust has agreed to look at it in the spring rather than find
out which kind of night it is going to be.
