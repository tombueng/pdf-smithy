# What is in here

This review is written once a year, in the fortnight after the last boat of
the season leaves and before the weather closes the crossing for the winter.
It is meant to be read by three kinds of people: the members of the trust,
who pay for the roof; the visiting ringers and surveyors, who want to know
what happened while they were away; and whoever inherits the wardenship,
who will find that every number in it has a story behind it.

## The sections

1. **Foreword.** Why the season was shorter than usual, and what that cost.
2. **The season in brief.** Landings, weather, and the state of the buildings.
3. **Four plates.** A photograph of the north shore, the ringing totals as a
   chart, a drawing of the tide gauge, and a study of the paint we now use on
   the boat house.
4. **The year end to end.** One wide sheet with every week on it.
5. **A page that arrived turned.** Left as it came, on purpose.
6. **The visitor permit.** Two pages of form. Fill it in, do not print it.
7. **A page with remarks on it.** What a marked-up draft looks like.
8. **Colophon.** How this document was put together.

## A note on the numbers

Bird counts are the warden's, taken at the same three points on the same
walk, in the first two hours after sunrise. Weather is from the station's own
gauge on the roof of the store, which is not a certified instrument and has
never pretended to be. Where a figure is estimated it says so.
