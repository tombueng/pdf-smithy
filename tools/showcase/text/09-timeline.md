# The year, end to end

A wide sheet, because the year is wider than it is tall. Each row is a month
of the season and each column a thing worth counting. The sheet is set on
paper turned the long way round, which is a different matter from a page that
has been turned after the fact; the next page in this review is one of those,
and the two are worth comparing.

Ten months, eighty-eight boat days, forty-one visitors, one hundred and
sixty-two nights, eight hundred and thirty-three birds ringed, and six
hundred and eleven millimetres of rain. The first row is the one to look at
twice: two boat days and nothing else, because there was nowhere to land.
